Reading the sphere

In a multifactor model, the population factor loadings span a subspace B. Principal component analysis (PCA) gives us an estimate H of that subspace. The figure shows the one-factor case, where each can be drawn as a unit direction. Think of z, a unit direction chosen in advance, as the north pole. If z carries information about B, the latitude of B stays away from the equator as the dimension grows. The dotted circle marks that latitude.

Starting at H, how far should we move toward z? In the figure, the best stopping point is H*, the point on their great circle closest to B. To construct it, project B orthogonally onto the plane spanned by H and z, then scale the projection to unit length. The right angle marks where the arc from B meets the great circle.

In a Euclidean triangle, Stewart’s theorem lets us use the three side lengths to locate the foot of an altitude. On the unit sphere, the three side lengths are the angles between B, H, and z. They determine where H* lies. We can measure the angle between H and z directly. The statistical problem is to estimate the two angles involving the unobserved B.

Alex Shkolnik and I develop the multifactor correction in The Quadratic Optimization Bias of Large Covariance Matrices.
