Olga Radko Endowed Math Circle

The circle is a free mathematics enrichment program at UCLA for K–12 students across Greater Los Angeles. Olga Radko founded it in 2007 as the Los Angeles Math Circle. Today it carries her name.

A Sunday at the circle

During the academic year, students gather in UCLA’s Mathematical Sciences Building. In small groups, they draw diagrams, try examples, and explain to one another why an idea works. An instructor helps them think through the next question.

The questions grow with the students. A beginning group might count squares of different sizes on a chessboard. Older groups explore subjects such as number theory, cryptography, and mathematical proof.

A problem from the circle

Two crossing lines divide a sheet of paper into four regions. How many regions can you make with three lines? With ten? Imagine extending each line across the whole plane, with every pair crossing and each crossing point distinct.

This problem comes from an Intermediate 2A session on induction. Drawing the first few pictures suggests a pattern. Counting how many new regions appear each time you add one line gives a way to prove it.
